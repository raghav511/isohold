Guide

Each hold is followed by a rep exercise for the same muscles. The hold strengthens the tendon; the reps, taken close to failure, build muscle. Every exercise comes round twice a week. Each day is split into a morning and an evening session of about 20 to 35 minutes, 60 minutes at most.

30–45 sTendons like long, heavy tension

Tendons adapt to how hard and how long they are strained. Holds of 30–45 seconds at about 70% of your best effort raise tendon stiffness more than quick reps. Reviews of training studies find that holds at 70% effort or more make tendons stiffer and stronger, and holds at long muscle lengths build the most muscle. An early 6-person study suggested holds also relieve tendon pain instantly, but larger trials since then found ordinary reps work just as well for pain.

AM + PMWhy two short sessions

Tendon collagen responds strongly to a short bout of loading, then goes quiet for several hours. Two sessions 6 or more hours apart give it two signals instead of one, and each session stays short enough to fit around a workday. The morning starts with a 5-minute treadmill walk as a warm-up; the evening ends on the rower.

AlternateWhy exercises are grouped

Exercises in a group work different muscles, so you go from one to the next with only about 15 seconds to set up. Each muscle still gets about a minute of rest while you work the others. This saves around a third of the session time compared with resting between every set. Each exercise's holds still come right before its rep sets.

125–150 minCardio across the week

The warm-up walks, rowing finishers, and the Wednesday and Saturday cardio days add up to roughly 125–150 minutes a week, close to the common 150-minute health guideline. On the busiest strength days the warm-up and rowing finisher shrink so the day stays within an hour. Most of it is "zone 2": you can still talk in full sentences. The short hard rowing intervals in the Build and Heavy phases build fitness without taking time from the strength work. The rower also trains the legs, back and grip, so keep easy days genuinely easy.

PairsWhy each hold has a partner

Holds build tendon stiffness and strength at specific angles, but they are a weaker signal for muscle size. Slow reps through the full range, stopped close to failure, are what grow muscle. Doing them straight after the hold, for the same muscles, means tendon and muscle are trained in step, so muscle strength does not outrun what the tendon can handle.

0–3 RIRHow hard the reps should be

RIR means reps in reserve: how many more you could have done. Growth comes mainly from the last few hard reps, so the target RIR drops from 3 in weeks 1–2 to 1–2 in weeks 6–11. Pick a weight that lands you inside the rep range at that RIR. When you beat the top of the range on both sets, go heavier next week.

2–3 sLowering slowly

Each rep lowers over 2–3 seconds and pauses briefly in the stretched position. Slow lowering under load is also good for tendons, and the stretched position is where muscle growth is strongest. The timer animates the tempo and counts reps for you; tap "Set done" if you reach your limit early.

1.6 g/kgEat for growth

Muscle needs material. Aim for roughly 1.6 g of protein per kg of body weight a day, spread over 3–4 meals, and enough total food that your weight holds steady or rises slowly. Sleep of 7–9 hours matters as much as the training for both muscle and tendon repair.

±20°Strength sticks to the angle

An isometric hold mostly strengthens the joint angle you train, plus roughly 15–30° either side. That is why the Heavy phase alternates between the mid-range (step 2) and end position (step 3), and why many holds here sit in a stretched position, which carries over best.

RPEChoosing the weight
  • RPE 6: steady, could hold twice as long.
  • RPE 7–8: the last 10 s are hard, form is perfect.
  • RPE 9: you could hold only 2–5 s more.

If shaking breaks your position, end the set. When you finish every set at the target RPE, add about 5–10% next session.

48–72 hRecovery between the same region

Collagen building in a tendon stays raised for about two to three days after a heavy session. Each exercise comes round twice a week, about 72 hours apart: upper body and core on days 1 and 4, lower body on days 2 and 5. Wednesday is a cardio-only day, so there is a break from heavy loading between day 2 and day 3.

BreatheNever hold your breath

Holding your breath during a long hold spikes blood pressure. Breathe in through the nose and out through pursed lips, keeping the brace. If you have high blood pressure or a heart condition, check with your doctor before heavy isometrics.

≤ 3/10The pain rule

Mild tendon discomfort up to 3 out of 10 that settles by the next morning is acceptable. Sharp pain, pain that grows during the hold, or pain worse the next day means go lighter or shorter. For an existing injury, work with a physiotherapist.

SingleIsolating a stubborn tendon

If one tendon bothers you (knee, Achilles, elbow), keep loading it at a level that stays within the pain rule: wall sit for the knee, seated calf hold for the Achilles, overhead triceps hold for the back of the elbow. Holds and slow reps both work; pick whichever you tolerate better. Holds are not a quick fix for pain, so for pain that lasts more than a few weeks, see a physiotherapist.

ColorsThe phase colors

Phases borrow the competition kettlebell color code: 16 kg yellow, 20 kg purple, 24 kg green, 28 kg orange, 32 kg red. The weights are a reminder that load climbs through the 12 weeks, not a prescription. Use what fits your RPE.
